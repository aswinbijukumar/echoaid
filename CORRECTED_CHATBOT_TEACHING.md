# EchoAid ISL Teaching Guide

## Practice Tips and Techniques
**The Dominant Hand Rule**: Use the hand you write with for the "active" part of the sign. For letters like M or N, your dominant fingers should touch your non-dominant palm.

**Visual Shapes**: Many ISL signs are designed to look like the letter they represent. For example, D and P use an index finger as the "stem" and the other hand to create the "loop".

**Mirror Practice**: Practice in front of a mirror to ensure your hand alignment matches the images precisely, especially for interlocking signs like S (pinkies hooked).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- **Confusing I and 1**: In ISL, the sign for the letter I and the number 1 are identical (vertical index finger), so context is important.
- **Incorrect Finger Count**: For letters like M (three fingers) and N (two fingers), using the wrong number of fingers on the palm will change the letter entirely.
- **Palm Orientation**: For numbers 1-5, ensure your palm is facing outward toward the person you are "speaking" to.

## Learning Strategies
- **Group by Shape**: Learn signs that use similar motions together. For example, M, N, and R all involve placing fingers against the opposite palm.
- **Finger Spelling**: Once you know A-Z, start spelling simple words. This helps with the transition speed between signs.
- **Visual Association**: Remember B as "spectacles" and W as "interlocked fingers" to make memorization easier.
## Technical Optimization Tips (Performance & Accuracy)
- **Lighting & Background**: Use a plain background. Ensure light hits hands from the front. Shadows on the palm can cause misidentification (especially for G or W).
- **Frame Alignment**: Keep both hands fully within the frame. If too close, the model cannot see all landmarks.
- **Confidence thresholds**: If confidence < 85%, tilt hands slightly towards the camera for better depth detection.

## ISL-Specific "Legit" Coaching
- **The "Dominant Hand" Rule**: Keep your non-dominant hand steady as the base. Use your dominant hand for the movement.
- **Common Confusion Alerts**:
    - **N vs V/U**: Ensure the ring finger is tucked in for 'N'.
    - **I vs 1**: Identical sign; context matters.
- **Transition Fluidity**: Don't snap between signs. Focus on the flow to build muscle memory.

## Gamification & Engagement
- **Feedback Style**: Be specific (e.g., "Great finger loops on that 'B'!").
- **Did You Know?**: ISL 'Z' uses the side of the hand, unlike ASL's air-drawing.

## Error Handling & Stability
- **Jitter Reduction**: Rest elbows on a table if hands are shaking.
- **Difficult Signs**: If a user fails 3 times, suggest watching the slow-motion video in the dictionary.
